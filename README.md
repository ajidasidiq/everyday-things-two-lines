A program that writes a two-line explanation of an everyday thing — a traffic jam — to a 10-year-old, built on one concrete, surprising analogy, with an honest second line about what the analogy maps and what it leaves out.

## The entry

> A traffic jam is a whole river of cars trying to squeeze through the neck of a ketchup bottle all at once.
>
> It maps the many-cars-one-narrow-spot squeeze that makes everything crawl, but it leaves out the drivers, the traffic lights, and the fact that cars can turn off down another road while ketchup cannot choose.

Line 1 explains the traffic jam through a single vivid picture a 10-year-old can hold: everyone knows what happens when ketchup has to come out of a narrow bottle neck, slow dribble instead of a rush. Line 2 is honest about the mapping: it covers the squeeze that makes everything crawl, and it names what it leaves out — drivers who choose, traffic lights, and alternate routes, none of which ketchup has.

## Run it

```sh
python3 main.py
```

Writes the two lines to `/output/entry.txt` (or `$OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt`), with a self-check that the entry is exactly two lines, one sentence each, of 1–400 characters.
